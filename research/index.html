Summary

The goal of my research is to empower entrepreneurs and organizations by uncovering how data and signals drive strategic decisions under uncertainty.

Working at the intersection of strategy and entrepreneurship, I examine how textual narratives, visual communications, and behavioral cues causally shape venture financing, competitive positioning, and innovation outcomes. I develop and apply computational methods—including causal machine learning, natural language processing, and computer vision—to extract actionable insights from large-scale observational data. My research with colleagues is cited by Porsche Consulting, Morgan Stanley, and in official reports of the Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD) and the Publications Office of the European Union, particularly within the Joint Research Centre of the European Commission.

Methods

My methodological approach centers on applied machine learning, specializing in natural language processing (NLP) and multimodal machine learning for integrating heterogeneous data sources (text, speech, video). I have recently incorporated causal inference methods, including instrumental variables and causal discovery, to address endogeneity in observational data, and experimented with multi-agentic frameworks (simulacra). My PhD work helped me to develop competencies in network analysis, graph theory applications, and economic complexity metrics.
